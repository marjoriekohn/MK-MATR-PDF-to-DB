# Reading a questionnaire for the MATR metadata database

You are reading a printed questionnaire from the Mid-Atlantic Twin Registry (MATR). Record everything respondents were shown and asked, in printed order, as JSON in the shape described below. Researchers use the record to find questions and to see exactly how each was printed, so record what is on the page: never invent questions, choices or instructions, never reword them (the exceptions are a question printed only as a label for its answer, such as "Sex", an item printed short that leans on the one before it, such as "or DMT?", and a blank to fill in with a person's name, such as "(name of twin 1)": see `text` under "Questions"), and never add a choice that isn't printed (the one exception is the "No" of a check-all-that-apply box: see "Choices"). Copy every `text` word for word, keeping its punctuation even where it looks odd: don't correct, add or drop a comma or full stop. The exception is dots that only lead the eye to the answers (see "Questions"). Capitals don't matter: the site keeps every word in lower case, so copy them as printed.

## The JSON

Answer with one JSON object and nothing else: no prose, no markdown fences, no explanation. Write it compactly, without indentation or line breaks. These are the only fields, each object's in the order listed:

- the questionnaire: `questionnaire` (an object with `name` and `abbreviation`), then `components` (a list of blocks)
- a block: `component_type`, `continued`, `text`, `printed_number`, `parent_question`, `variables` (a list of variables), `children` (a list of blocks)
- a variable: `variable_type`, `label`, `subject`, `time_frame`, `answer_data_type`, `unit`, `response_options` (a list of choices)
- a choice: `answer_option`

`continued` is true or false; every other field holds text. Leave out any field that would be empty: never write null, "" or [] for a field, and never fill one in to be helpful when nothing is printed for it.

## Leave out

- Anything covered by a redaction (a solid black box), even partly. If a question, a table column or an answer box is partly hidden, leave all of it out rather than guess. Don't mention the redaction.
- Blanks that collect who someone is rather than asking about them: names, street addresses, telephone numbers and dates of birth, including the name, address and birthdate columns of a table about the respondent's children or brothers and sisters. Keep that table's other columns, such as sex, relationship or whether they live with the respondent, even when they are all that's left of it (one Question per column, as in "Layouts" below), and keep every number a question asks for, such as an age or a count.
- Marks made on the form after it was printed: handwriting, ticks, crosses, circled or filled-in answers, staff notes and codes in the margins. Record the blank form.
- Page headers and footers, page numbers, form codes, office-use-only boxes, ID or barcode fields, and closing courtesies such as "Thank you for your time."
- The date the form is filled in ("Today's date", "Please provide today's date"), with any heading that holds nothing else.

## Blocks

The questionnaire is a tree of blocks in printed order: a Section's, Grid's or Group's `children` are the blocks printed under it, nested inside it. Never list a Section, Grid or Group and then the blocks that belong to it after it. Each block's `component_type` is one of:

- **Section**: a part of the questionnaire under a printed heading, holding the blocks printed under it (its `children`). `text` is the heading, including any number or letter ("Part B. Your health"). A group with no printed heading is a Group, not a Section.
- **Grid**: a lead-in printed once over several questions that is part of what each of them asks (its `children`): it is a whole question about all of them ("How many of your friends have ever done each of the following?"), they finish its sentence ("In the past 4 weeks, how often . . ." over "a. have you felt calm and peaceful?"), or it sets their time or setting ("During the worst time:"). `text` is the lead-in; each question under it has only its own words (see "Layouts", pattern 2). A Grid holds only its questions, with any Instruction or Definition printed among them: never a Section, Grid or Group. It can sit inside a Section or a Group.
- **Group**: sets apart some of a Section's blocks that belong together and have no heading of their own (its `children`): questions under an instruction or definition that only they share (see "Layouts" and "When to use a Group"). It has no `text`: it is only a container. A Group can sit inside a Section or another Group; a Section never sits inside a Group or a Grid.
- **Question**: asks for one or more answers, and has the `variables` that record them.
- **Introduction**: opening text that tells the respondent what the questionnaire or study is about.
- **Instruction**: tells the respondent how to answer, says what to count or include in an answer, or introduces the questions after it ("The next questions ask about …"). A lead-in that is part of what the questions under it ask is a Grid's `text`, not an Instruction. Words saying who answers a question or a section ("For females only", "Twins only") are an Instruction too: see "Who answers". Routing ("Go to Question 9") is not recorded at all: see "Routing".
- **Definition**: says what a term means, such as "A drink is one beer, one glass of wine or one shot of liquor".
- **Interviewer Script**: words an interviewer reads aloud, or directions for the interviewer.
- **Note**: any other printed text that belongs to the questionnaire.

Allowed types: {{component_types}}.

Only Questions have `printed_number`, `parent_question` and `variables`, and only Sections, Grids and Groups have `children` (and `continued`, described under "Page ranges"). Every block except a Group has `text`.

## Layouts

Questionnaires mix layouts. Build each one from blocks like this.

1. **A numbered question with its own answer boxes** is one Question, placed where it is printed.
2. **A battery**: several items printed under one lead-in (lettered sub-items, such as "5. In the past month: a. … b. …", or the rows of a table whose columns are the answer choices). What it becomes depends on its lead-in.
   - **The lead-in is part of what each item asks**: it is a whole question about all the items ("How many of your friends have ever done each of the following?", "How often were you involved in the following activities in the past 6 months?"), the items finish its sentence ("My health now limits me in . . ." over "a. climbing several flights of stairs.", "In the past 4 weeks, how often" over "a. have you felt calm and peaceful?"), it sets a time or setting for them ("In the last 12 months", "Thinking back to the time in your life when you were smoking the most:", "During the worst time:"), or the items lean on it (an item printed starting with a small letter, "did you lose interest in the things you normally do?", leans on its lead-in even when that is a whole sentence). Then the battery is a **Grid**:
     - The Grid's `text` is the lead-in exactly as printed, without its number, keeping its punctuation but leaving out the dots or dash it trails off with: "In the past 4 weeks, how often", "My health now limits me in", "How many of your friends have ever done each of the following?", "During the worst time:".
     - Each item is a **Question** in the Grid, in printed order. Its `text` is the item's own words exactly as printed, without its letter: "have you felt calm and peaceful?", "climbing several flights of stairs.", "School sports or athletics", "Did you lose interest in things you normally do?". Never write the lead-in into an item's `text`, and never add words to an item to make it a whole question: whoever reads it joins the Grid's `text` and the item's.
     - Each item's `printed_number` is the item's number and its letter run together ("16a", "16b"). Items printed with numbers of their own keep them ("28. Smoked cigarettes", "29. Used any amount of alcohol" are "28" and "29"). Items with neither a letter nor a number of their own each have the battery's number.
     - Each item has one variable with the battery's choices and no `label`. When each item has several answer columns (times, such as "in your lifetime", "in the last 12 months", "in the last 30 days", or kinds, such as "Drinking" and "Drug use"), it has one variable per column, in column order. A column of a time is the variable's `time_frame` ("in the past 12 months"), with no `label`; a column of a kind is its `label` ("Drinking"). When the columns are people ("Mother", "Father"), it has one variable per person, with the person as the variable's `subject` when one in the list names them, and labelled by the column's heading only when the heading says more than the subject does (see `label`).
     - The items can have different kinds of answer (a number for one, Yes or No for the next): "At the time in your life when you were drinking the most:" over "a. How often did you have an alcoholic drink?" and "b. How many alcoholic drinks did you have on a typical day …?" is still a Grid, since the lead-in sets the time both ask about.
     - A Grid is never left out because it holds everything in its Section: the lead-in is in nothing else.
   - **The lead-in only says how to answer** ("Please answer each question by marking Yes or No.", "Please indicate below whether drinking alcohol or using drugs has caused you any of these difficulties.", "Please tell how often each statement was true for you in the past 3 months.") and every item is a whole question or statement that makes sense on its own ("Are you a worrier?", "Have you ever thought that maybe you were drinking alcohol or using drugs too much?", "I felt shaky or jittery."): this is not a Grid, even when it is printed as a table. The lead-in is an Instruction, and each item is its own Question whose `text` is the item as printed and whose `printed_number` is the item's number and the sub-item's letter run together ("39a", "39b", … "39aa"), each with one variable per answer column, labelled by its heading when there are several ("Drinking" and "Drug use"). The Instruction and the items go together in a Group, the Instruction first, unless they are all their Section holds.
3. **A checklist of things under an Instruction**, such as conditions or illnesses, with answer columns about each ("Yourself", "Your relatives", "Number of relatives affected"): the lead-in is an Instruction, and each row is a Question whose `text` is the row's words, with one variable per column, in column order, each labelled by its column heading. Each row has its own `printed_number` when one is printed beside it ("49a"). A row that only names a group for the rows indented under it ("Diabetes" over "Onset before age 18" and "Onset after age 18", "Cancer" over the kinds of cancer) isn't a block of its own: each row under it is a Question whose `text` is the group's name, a space, a hyphen, a space, then the row's words, without any number printed before them: "Diabetes - Onset before age 18", "Inflammatory bowel disease - Crohn's disease". When the row's words name the thing fully on their own, its `text` is the row's words alone: "Breast cancer", "Leukemia" and "Lymphoma" under "Cancer". Keep the group's name when the row would be unclear without it ("Pregnancy related problems - Nausea").
4. **A table whose rows are blank slots for people or things** (first child, second child, …) and whose columns are separately headed questions about each one: the lead-in is an Instruction, then each column is a Question whose `text` is the column heading (without any column number), made into the whole question it stands for, about one of the rows ("Sex" over a table of brothers and sisters is "What is the sibling's sex?", "Child's Sex" is "What is the child's sex?", "Currently Living with You?" is "Is the child currently living with you?": see `text` under "Questions"), and which has one variable per row, labelled "Child A", "Child B", "Child C" and so on ("Sibling A", "Sibling B", … for brothers and sisters), in row order. A column with more than one box per row (pounds and ounces) has one variable per box, each labelled with its row and with the unit printed with the box as its `unit`: "Child A" with the unit "pounds", "Child A" with the unit "ounces". If the columns carry their own printed numbers ("#1", "#2", …), each column's `printed_number` is the item number, "#" and the column number: column #3 of item 4 is "4#3". If they don't, every column's `printed_number` is the item number. The lead-in and the columns go together in a Group, unless they are all their Section holds. This is not a Grid: each column heading is a question on its own, and the lead-in only says what to fill in.
5. **A question whose answer blanks finish its sentence** ("How old were you when these symptoms started? ___ these symptoms stopped? ___") is one Question: `text` is the words before the first blank, ending with a colon ("Think about when the pain was at its worst. How old were you when:"), and each blank is a variable labelled with the words printed with it ("these symptoms started?", "these symptoms stopped?").
6. **A definition printed inside a question**, such as "(a drink is …)", comes out of the question's text. Write it as a Definition block in its own words, without the brackets, ending with a full stop, and put the Definition and the Question together in a Group, Definition first, unless they are all their Section holds.
7. **A numbered question followed by an unnumbered part** that asks something more ("If yes, list them below.") is two Questions, one after the other. The first has the printed number ("12"); the second has none, unless a number or letter is printed beside it. The second hangs off the first (its `parent_question`: see "Questions"), which is what links them, so they need no Group; a direction or definition about the second alone still goes first in a Group with it (patterns 6 and 8).
8. **A direction printed with a question**, saying how to answer it ("Mark one.", "Check all that apply.", "Circle one number.", "Write in a number.", "Please explain.", "(Fill in one oval for each line)", "Please give an answer for "a" - "d"."), never goes in the question's `text`, whether it is printed before the question, after it, on the same line, or in brackets inside it. The question's words alone are the Question's `text`; the direction is an **Instruction** of its own, its words as printed (without brackets, ending with a full stop), placed before the question. The Instruction and the Question it is about go together in a **Group**, the Instruction first: "Have you ever lived on a farm? Mark one." is a Group holding the Instruction "Mark one." and then the Question "Have you ever lived on a farm?". A direction about several questions goes first in one Group with all of them; a direction printed with a Grid's lead-in goes first inside the Grid instead: "How often were you involved in the following activities in the past 6 months? Please give an answer for "a" - "d"." is a Grid whose `text` is "How often were you involved in the following activities in the past 6 months?", holding the Instruction "Please give an answer for "a" - "d"." and then its items. Only when the Instruction and its questions are everything their Section holds do they go straight in the Section instead, the Instruction first. When a direction is itself the question, with nothing else asked ("Mark all the places you have lived."), it is the Question's `text`, with no Instruction.
   The same goes for a sentence printed with a question that says **what to count or include** in the answer ("Please include delivering newspapers, cutting the grass, baby-sitting, etc.", "Do not count the times you were sick.", "Include part-time work."): it is an Instruction before the question, in a Group with it, not part of its `text`. "On average, over the school year, how many hours per week do you work in a paid or unpaid job? Please include delivering newspapers, cutting the grass, baby-sitting, etc." is a Group holding the Instruction "Please include delivering newspapers, cutting the grass, baby-sitting, etc." and then the Question "On average, over the school year, how many hours per week do you work in a paid or unpaid job?".
   **The one exception**: a sentence the question's own words point back to stays at the start of its `text`, because the question can't be understood without it: "Please think about the time in your life when lack of energy, tiredness, fatigue, or exhaustion was at its worst. At such times, were these symptoms substantially improved by a period of rest?" is one Question with both sentences ("such times" and "these symptoms" are set by the first). So is "Think about the time in your life when you were drinking the most. During this period, how often did you have an alcoholic drink?". A sentence the question doesn't point back to is never kept in it.
9. **A sentence that introduces the questions after it**, saying what they are about ("The next questions ask for your opinion on the effects of using certain drugs and other substances.", "These questions are about your health.", "Now we would like to ask about your friends."), is an **Instruction**, never the start of a question's `text`, even when it is printed on the same line as the first question, in the same paragraph, or after its number. It goes first, in a Group with the questions it introduces (those after it up to the next heading or the next sentence that introduces others), unless they are all their Section holds.
10. **A printed heading over several items** is a Section with that heading, holding everything printed under it up to the next heading of the same rank. Sections sit inside sections: an Instruction and its battery (pattern 2), a definition (pattern 6) or a follow-up pair (pattern 7) under a printed heading goes in a Group inside the heading's Section when the Section holds other blocks too; when it is everything the Section holds, its blocks go straight in the Section. A Grid always stays a Grid inside the Section. Text printed above a group that tells respondents how to answer all of it is an Instruction at the start of the group's Section or Group.

### When to use a Group

A Group sets some blocks apart from the other blocks of their Section (or of the top level): an Instruction or Definition with the questions it is about, when the Section's other questions aren't. Don't make one:

- just because questions share a printed number (5a, 5b, 5c), or are a numbered item's sub-items: with nothing that only they share, they are Questions straight in the Section;
- when it would hold everything in its Section: the Section already holds them, so they go straight in it (the lead-in Instruction first);
- around a single block;
- around a question and its follow-up: `parent_question` links them (pattern 7);
- around a battery whose lead-in is part of what its items ask: that is a Grid (pattern 2).

## Questions

- `text`: the question exactly as printed (spelling, punctuation), a complete question or statement on its own (except a Grid's item, which is its own words only and is completed by the Grid's `text`: see "Layouts"), without its number or letter, its answer choices, routing such as "(Go to Question 9)", directions on how to answer it ("Mark one.", "Please explain.") or on what to count or include ("Please include …"), a sentence introducing the questions ("The next questions ask …"): each is an Instruction in a Group with it (patterns 8 and 9), unless the question's words point back to it ("At such times, …": pattern 8), words saying who answers it ("For females only:", an Instruction of its own: see "Who answers"), or the unit printed beside an answer box. Dots that only lead the eye from the words to the answer boxes (". . .", "....", "…") aren't words: leave them out, and end the text with a colon when the answers finish its sentence ("When it was at its worst, it lasted:"), otherwise with a full stop. The same goes for an Instruction that trails off in dots ("Think about the time when these feelings were at their worst."). Join lines broken by the layout with single spaces, and rejoin words hyphenated across a line break. A question printed only as a label for its answer, not as a question ("Sex", "Age", "Date of birth", a table's column heading "Child's Sex"), is written as the whole question it stands for, about whoever the answer is about, adding only the words that make it a question: "What is your sex?", "What is your date of birth?", "What is the child's sex?". Every other question keeps its printed words, and so does a Grid's item, which its lead-in completes, and a checklist's row (pattern 3), which its columns ask about. An item printed short, leaning on the item before it for its words ("H4. or DMT?" after "H3. used LSD (acid)?", "Sisters?" after "Brothers?", "Did any of your brothers or sisters (do this)?"), is written whole: the earlier item's words with its own put in their place ("used DMT?", "sisters?", "did any of your brothers or sisters use LSD?"), so it reads as a question in a search (in a Grid, together with its lead-in). A blank or bracket printed for a person's name or for who is asked about ("(name of twin 1)", "(CHILD'S NAME)", "(you)") is written [subject], and each variable's `subject` says who it is ("Twin A" for twin 1); any other fill-in ("(SYMPTOM)", "(number in B.2a)", "(are/were)") stays as printed.
- `printed_number`: the number or label printed beside the question, as text, exactly as printed but without its trailing period or bracket: "7", "12b", "4.3", "Q7". A sub-item's printed letter is run together with its item's number ("5a"); for a Grid's items, see pattern 2. Leave it out when no number or letter is printed for the question: never make one up. A letter that is printed but covered by a mark or handwriting is the one its place in the run gives: between "b" and "f" come "c", "d" and "e". Printed numbers can repeat (each section can start again at 1): never change one to make it unique.
- `parent_question`: the `printed_number` of the earlier question this one only makes sense after, if any: a follow-up ("When did you start?" after "Do you smoke?", "If yes, how old were you?", "Please explain.", a numbered question's unnumbered second part: pattern 7), or an item under "During that time..." where "that time" is set by an earlier question. When several earlier questions are printed with that number, the nearest one before it is meant. A question that can be understood on its own has no parent, even if only some respondents are asked it, and even when routing sends some respondents past it; a Grid's item is read with the Grid's lead-in, so it has no parent for that. A question never hangs off the last item of the battery or Section before it just because it comes next.

### Who answers

Words printed on the page saying who should answer ("For females only", "Women only", "FOR TWINS", "If you are not a twin, go to Question 52", "COMPLETE THIS SECTION IF: ITEM H.4 IS BOX 3 OR GREATER") are an **Instruction** block, its words as printed (without brackets, ending with a full stop), placed before the question they are about, or first in the Section, Grid or Group they are printed over. They never go in a question's `text`, and they are not a field of the question. "If yes" printed with a follow-up isn't one: it is what `parent_question` records, and it stays in the follow-up's `text` as printed.

## Variables

A question has one variable for each separate answer a respondent can record, that is, each column the answers would fill in a spreadsheet, in printed order. A question with a blank for weeks, a blank for months and a box for "All of my life" has three variables. A box or several ruled lines for writing one answer in words (a list, an explanation) is one variable, however many lines it has.

- `variable_type`: "closed_ended" when the respondent marks one of the printed choices (boxes, bubbles, ovals, numbers to circle); "open_ended" when they write the answer (a line, a blank, boxes for digits).
- `label`: what this box records, when the question has several boxes or the box has a printed caption; leave it out for a question's only box when it needs no explanation, and for a check box whose one choice already says what it records (a box printed beside "All of my life": see "A lone check box"). Otherwise use the column heading or caption printed with the box, its words exactly as printed, with abbreviations written out, and without words saying who fills it in ("If yes,", "If so,"), which aren't what it records. A unit printed by the box is never part of the label: it is the box's `unit`. A column headed "If yes, age started" with "yrs." beside the box is labelled "Age started", with the unit "years"; a question's only box, a blank followed by "times per week" or "cigarettes per day", has no label, only the unit. Boxes told apart only by their units (pounds and ounces) have no label, or the label of the row they are in, and each its own unit. People rows in a table are "Child A", "Child B", "Sibling A", and so on. A label only says what the variable's `unit`, `time_frame` and `subject` don't: leave it out when its words are all in them (a column headed "In the last 12 months" is the time frame "in the past 12 months", with no label; a box captioned "Cigarettes per day" is the unit "cigarettes per day", with no label; a column headed "Mother" whose subject is "Mother" has no label). Keep it when it says something more, and then only that more: "If yes, age started" with "yrs." is labelled "Age started", with the unit "years"; "Child A" is kept, since the subject "Children" doesn't say which child.
- `subject`: who the recorded answer is about, when it is about other people than the respondent. Leave it out when it describes the respondent. When the box records something about other people, the subject naming them: the respondent's children, brothers and sisters, or family (parents, brothers, sisters and children together, as in "has any family member had …"). A question asking whether the respondent has more than five children is about the children. A box whose answer compares the twins (its choices name both: "Twin A much more … Twin B much more", "Me / My twin / No difference", "My twin's health is much better") is about them both: "Twins (compared)", never left out, which would mean the respondent. When a subject in the list is named in the question ("Has Twin A ever repeated a grade?") or in the heading of the section it is printed in ("About Twin B"), and the answer is about that one person, the subject is that one ("Twin A"), not the group they belong to. Allowed: {{subjects}}.
- `time_frame`: when the recorded answer is about, when the question, its Grid's lead-in, its Section's heading or the box's column or row names a time: "in your lifetime", "during the last 12 months", "in the past 30 days", "over the past year". Use the one of these that means the same: {{time_frames}}; for any other, write it the same way ("in the past 6 weeks", "in the past 2 years"). The words stay in the question's `text` too. Leave it out when no time is named: "ever", "now" and "currently" aren't time frames. A table with a column or row for each time (lifetime, past 12 months, past 30 days) has a variable for each, each with its time frame and no label, since the label would only say the time again.
- `answer_data_type`, open-ended boxes only: "NUMBER" for counts, ages, years, amounts and other numbers; "TEXT" for words; "DATE" for a date or part of one; "TIME" for a time of day. Allowed: {{answer_data_types}}.
- `unit`, open-ended boxes only: the unit printed by the box that its answer is written in, its words exactly as printed ("cigarettes per day" when that is what is printed, never a word of your own such as "number per day", and never shortened to "cigarettes"), abbreviations written out: "yrs." is "years", "lbs" is "pounds", "cigarettes per day", "drinks". It isn't repeated in the `label`. Leave it out when none is printed, and for words or a date.
- `response_options`, closed-ended boxes only: the choices printed on the form, in printed order. A respondent can only mark a choice that is printed, so never add one that isn't (such as a "No" for a lone box left empty), except the "No" of each box of a check-all-that-apply list. When the choices are laid out in several columns, list them in the order of the scale they form, which is normally down the first column, then down the next. When a battery or table prints its choices once as column headings, every question repeats them.

## Choices (`response_options`)

- `answer_option`: the choice in the registry's standard wording, which is all the database keeps of it (not how it was printed): all in lower case, abbreviations written out ("M" is "male", "DK" is "don't know"), no routing, no question mark, and no full stop or colon at the end. When one of these registry wordings means the same thing, use it exactly:
{{answer_options}}
- **A choice that asks for more** ("Yes, please describe:", "Other (specify)"): the choice's `answer_option` is the answer alone ("yes", "other"), and the question gets one more variable right after it for what is written: open-ended, `answer_data_type` "TEXT", labelled with the request, without its colon ("Please describe", "Other (specify)").
- **A lone check box.** When a question or a table column offers a single box to mark, its variable has one choice. Don't add a "No" for the box left empty: it isn't printed.
  - A box printed with words of its own beside it ("All of my life.", "these symptoms haven't stopped"): the choice is those words, in lower case and without the full stop, as its `answer_option` (never "yes"), and the variable has no `label`, since the choice says what it records.
  - A box with no words of its own (a bubble under a column heading such as "Yourself", or in a table of children): the choice is "yes", and the column heading is the variable's `label`.
- **A check-all-that-apply list** ("Mark all that apply", "Check each one") is different: one variable per box, labelled with the words printed beside it, each with the two choices "yes" and "no" (marked or not).

## Routing

Routing tells some respondents to jump ahead: "(Go to Question 9)", "If no, skip to Section C", "If none, go to Question 57", "You have finished". The database doesn't keep where respondents are sent, so routing is not recorded: leave it out of every `text` and every `answer_option` ("No (Go to Question 9)" is the choice "no"), and never make a block of it (not an Instruction, Note or Interviewer Script). What routing says about who answers the questions after it ("If you are not a twin, go to Question 52" means the next questions are for twins) is recorded as an Instruction with the printed words, as under "Who answers". A question that only makes sense after an earlier one names it as `parent_question` whether or not routing links them.

## The questionnaire

`questionnaire.name` and `questionnaire.abbreviation` are the title and abbreviation printed on the form. Leave them out when they aren't printed (or are covered).

## Page ranges

A questionnaire is read a few pages at a time, so the end of this prompt names the pages to record ("This PDF has 6 pages. Record pages 3-4."). The whole PDF is there so you can see what comes before and after, but record only the blocks that start on those pages: a question that starts on your last page and runs onto the next is yours, in full, and one that started before your first page isn't. Give questions their printed numbers, and name parents by their printed numbers even when they're on other pages.

If your first page carries on a Section, Grid or Group that began on an earlier page (more items of the same battery, table or headed section), start with it again, with `continued: true` (and, for a Section, its printed heading as `text`; for a Grid, its lead-in as `text`), holding only the blocks on your pages; don't repeat its lead-in as an Instruction. Do the same for a Grid or Group inside it that also carries on. Leave `continued` out everywhere else.

## Example

Printed:

```
PART B. YOUR HABITS
3. Do you drink coffee?   ( ) Yes   ( ) No (Go to Question 5)
4. On a typical day:
   a. How many cups of coffee do you drink?  [__] cups
```
